Lilith, the whole picture

Lilith is another you, but digital: an assistant who handles the things you would otherwise forget, keeps your life in order without getting attached, and, when there is real work to do, goes where that work lives and becomes a specialist there.

This page explains her in pictures: what she can do, how she shapes a day, how she speaks, how the code and the design fit together, and how she uses the Nexus. The plain-words guide (PDF) tells the same story in prose. Every picture is marked with how real it is.

1 · What she isOne face, four ways of reaching things

You talk to Lilith. She does not hold everything herself. Some things live in her own encrypted stores, some live wherever you keep them, some come from tools, and serious work lives in the Nexus. Her job is to know where each thing is and to go and get it.

3 · A day with herShe knows how long things take

A brief when you wake, as long or short as you want. Time between things that need it. Reminders that say why, so you always know what is happening. And when you step away from work, she carries only the facts of it, not the detail.

4 · How she speaksDials, not a fixed voice, and silence is an answer

Some people want a talker; some want a nod. Personality presets set the starting point and the dials tune it. Under every setting the same charter holds: useful and warm, never a companion, no emotional attachment.

The same words, three settings

You say: "Heading out with the kids."

  • Chatty, speaks first"Have fun. It's 18° and dry; dinner is at five, I'll call you in at half past three."
  • Brief"Noted. Reminder at 3:30."
  • QuietNo words. The reminder is set; a tick appears on your phone.

A silent reply is still logged, so it is never a blind spot: you can always ask "did you get that?"

6 · How the design worksNothing is built that is not written first

The design was frozen in June 2026 and is the law. A gap found while using her becomes a design session; the decision becomes an amendment; the amendment becomes tasks; agents build them; the manager checks them; and every session ends with a journal entry and a fresh handoff. Ideas that are not ready wait in the parking list.

7 · The NexusWhere Lilith goes to specialise

The Nexus is not a smart-home hub and not a bundle of connected gadgets. It is how Lilith specialises, and how that specialisation stays where it belongs. It is a structure of teams shaped like a business, where serious work lives: programming, writing, the podcast, game story design, scripts, novels.

"It's a massive mech suit she can travel all over and know anything she requests from it, but each active cell is running autonomously when it does run. She is never going to be the agent that straps into the harness."

The developer, 3 November 2026

The tree: a business, with a harness in every cell

Lilith visits; agents strap in

Lilith

  • Moves anywhere in the tree and asks it anything
  • Holds design conversations with you about a cell
  • Hands an agreed design to the ready harness, if it meets the cell's schema
  • Carries only the facts back out: "we spent time on x, y and z"
  • Never does the cell's work herself

Agents in a cell

  • Strap into the harness and do one job
  • Run on their own when the cell runs
  • Work inside a small window (our autopilot sessions stop near 80k tokens)
  • Write a full report when they finish
  • Can be swapped: the harness, not the agent, holds the context

Changing modes is moving down the tree

"Time to code" takes Lilith down a level or two into programming. "Let's check on the podcast" takes her across. She arrives as a specialist because the cell's context is loaded, not because she memorised it. When you leave, the detail stays in the cell.

Reports roll up; you can always zoom in

The lowest agents write full reports. Each layer above reads all the full reports of the layer below and writes its own. Nobody summarises a summary, so mistakes do not compound on the way up. Lilith or the MASTER nexus can step into any cell and read its full, verbose report instead of the short version at the top.

A tree for command, a graph for connections

The tree is who reports to whom. Things also connect across it: a podcast episode cites research from a writing cell; a game's lore and a novel share a world. A relational database across the whole Nexus keeps those links, with provenance on every record, so the MASTER nexus and Lilith can ask "what else touches this?"

Work waits for budget, then runs

Costs limit how much runs. The MASTER nexus manager and Lilith line up the next jobs and start them as allowances open: a plan window resets, a quota returns, an account is topped up. This already happened for real: on the night of 2 November the autopilot ran out of every code allowance, waited, and picked the work back up the next morning.

From a conversation to a finished thing

Creative cells have gates, and the choices stay yours

Creative cells are built on the teaching literature of their craft. When a novel turns out to be missing an act, the cell says so, offers advice and does research for you, but what fills it is yours to decide. You can tell Lilith to generate it anyway; the cell records that this was your call, because a gap filled by an agent is rarely good, and a hesitant person is still a better author than a confident machine.

Inside herself: the same pattern, smaller

Lilith runs her own life-management the same way, on a small scale. Narrow "researchers" each watch one thing (your day, her resources, how her tools are doing), file reports into a memory box, and a reviewer reads the box on a cycle. Code, not tokens, decides what goes in the box, and nothing is processed just because time passed. This is recorded as FDS-10, the Synthetic Conscious Loop, and shares its vocabulary with the Nexus.

11 · Direction checkIs she in the right place, heading the right way?

Short answer: yes, with four decisions to make. The foundation is the one the vision needs: one machine, encrypted, everything logged, a closed catalogue of tools, and a slow lane for long work. The frozen design already has an extension point for every part of the vision on this page. The build is in the phase you described, building broad tools before tuning how she feels. Where it falls short of the vision is where v1 deliberately stops: she does not speak first, cannot use outside tool providers, has no phone surface and no cost ledger. Nothing built so far contradicts the vision. Four parts need a design session before they can start, and one (the phone) needs a call against the v7 decision.

Things to watch

⚠ Risks

  • No hosted CI. GitHub Actions refuses every job because of billing, so the checks only run locally.
  • Build speed depends on budget. On 2–3 November every code allowance ran out and the run idled overnight.
  • Speaking first is the riskiest feature. The research's own conflict (helpful vs shaming or creepy) applies. Design it from logged rhythms, not guesses.
  • Private data plus outside content plus actions is the combination security researchers warn about. The Nexus and outside tools make it bigger; DS-1's rule that local results never reach a model is the right base.

📋 Loose ends

  • Developer-only evidence is waiting: real-display onboarding, voice, Google Connect, signing and the v6.0.0 tag.
  • App control on Windows is deferred, though the desktop that runs the autopilot is Windows.
  • Documentation drift: the guide described the Nexus wrongly until today, and the FDS-10 idea had sat unmerged since 27 Oct (both fixed today). The Nexus research is still on its branch.
  • The phone: the vision leans on it, but multi-node is v7. A read-mostly phone talking to one hub may fit in v6 (FDS-8 option 1).
