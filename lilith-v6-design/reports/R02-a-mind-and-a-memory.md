# Report 02 — A Mind and a Memory (CH-03, CH-04)

*Covers Chapter CH-03 (Intelligence Core — §12, §23, §14, §13) and Chapter CH-04 (Memory & the First Tool — §18, §15, §24). This is the stretch where Lilith starts thinking, remembering, and — crucially — protecting the user while she does it.*

---

## Where we are in the story

The skeleton stands and it hears and speaks, but nothing connects the ears to the mouth. This stretch builds what goes between: the router that decides what kind of thought a moment calls for, the context manager that decides what she should know when she thinks it, the privacy layer that stands guard at the network boundary, the personality that makes the words hers, the memory that makes conversations accumulate into a relationship, and the very first tool — the seed of the entire artifact layer.

This is also the stretch where the deepest lessons of the project's history come due. The two-mode split exists because of the 47-second wall documented in the June 2026 blog post. The context window manager is the one idea that survived from the very first version of this project, four years ago, finally implemented against a real design. And the PII tokenization layer is the genesis post's founding promise — *sanitized, necessary data only* — turning from conviction into code.

## What we're building, in plain English

**CH-03 builds the mind's plumbing, text-only.** Type a sentence in; a deterministic set of rules instantly decides whether this is *conversation* (a quick spoken exchange that must feel immediate) or an *artifact* (real work that should happen in a separate lane). The context manager assembles what Lilith knows — who she is from `EDICTS.md` and `VOICE.md`, plus the recent back-and-forth, capped hard at 1,200 tokens so conversation stays fast. Before any of it leaves the machine, a detector sweeps the outgoing payload for names, emails, and phone numbers and swaps them for anonymous tags; when Claude's streamed reply echoes a tag back, it's swapped to the real thing on the fly before anyone sees or hears it. The reply streams in Lilith's voice, shaped by her personality files.

**CH-04 gives the mind a past.** Every turn of conversation is written into the encrypted database and indexed for full-text search. The tool system arrives with exactly one tool in its catalog: `recall_memory`, which searches that history. And with the first tool comes the narrative layer — the acknowledge ("let me look"), the handoff ("here's what you said"), and the failure line she speaks when something goes wrong, all defined in the tool's own YAML file. Ask "what did I say yesterday about the concrete mixing ratio?" and the whole spine fires: route → acknowledge → search → narrate the answer.

## What we'll be thinking about

**The fork is the product.** The two-mode router looks like a small piece of code — keyword rules, no LLM in the path — but it encodes v6's central architectural thesis: *a spoken conversation and a unit of real work have incompatible needs, and pretending otherwise ruins both.* Conversation needs sub-3-second responses and short answers; work needs time, tools, and unbounded output. Everything downstream (the narrative layer, the artifact lane, eventually local overnight processing) depends on this fork being made early and cheaply. We will build it knowing it is a *hypothesis* — CH-08 exists specifically to try to falsify it against a steelmanned single-path alternative, and the rules we write here must not be tuned against that future test set. Build it clean, build it instrumented, and hold it loosely.

**The privacy layer is not optional equipment.** It would have been easy to design v1 with tokenization "coming later" — and the design reviews explicitly rejected that, amending the plan so that the PII pass ships *inside* Milestone 1 and the latency benchmark measures the pipeline as it will actually run. This is the right kind of paranoia: a privacy layer bolted on after launch is a privacy layer that fights every assumption already frozen around it. So in CH-03 we implement the detector and the substitution machinery as first-class pipeline stages: single-pass, streaming-aware, with the subtle cases handled precisely — a tag split across two stream chunks is held until it completes, and a tag the model invents that maps to nothing becomes a neutral placeholder rather than leaking raw into her voice or the database. The mental model to hold: **the network boundary is a border crossing, and nothing personal crosses without papers.** This is Lilith's version of the founding promise, and it's also her honesty made mechanical — what she sends away is provably less than what she knows.

**Personality is architecture, not decoration.** `EDICTS.md` (the rules she never breaks) and `VOICE.md` (the way she sounds) enter the system prompt in a fixed order, as stable blocks that the provider's prompt caching can hold onto. Two things matter here beyond the obvious. First, separation: rules, style, and (later) learned facts about the user are three different stores with three different lifecycles, and keeping them apart is what lets each evolve safely. Second, the v6 versions are *new documents* — v5's files are reference, not inheritance. Writing them is a small task and a big decision, because every response the system ever gives will pass through their filter.

**Memory begins humble, and that's a feature.** v1 recall is recency plus full-text search. No embeddings, no vector database, no semantic magic — not because those are bad, but because the design's own research flagged the encrypted-vector stack as an unproven bet, parked it behind a floating experiment (EXP-3), and committed to shipping something *provable* first. FTS5 over your own words, inside an encrypted database, answering "what did I say about X" — that's already the daily-use dream from the Milestone-1 objective, and it works with zero new dependencies. Restraint here buys certainty; the roadmap for hybrid recall is written down and gated, and we will not jump the gate. Part of CH-04's definition of done is literally an audit that no vector dependency crept in.

**The first tool sets the pattern for every tool.** `recall_memory` matters less for what it does than for what it establishes: a tool is a YAML definition plus a function; it runs through one runner; it declares its own spoken narratives; it will emit its own telemetry. The 80% principle from the introduction report starts here in miniature — the user asks a question in passing, and the system does the legwork (route, search, rank, compose) and returns a finished answer rather than a search interface. When there are twenty tools in the catalog someday, each one will be a copy of the shape we get right now, once.

**Narratives keep the conversation honest during work.** The acknowledge/handoff/failure lines are the seam where the two modes meet the user's ear. They're also where Lilith's honesty about her own nature first becomes audible: she *says* she's going to look, and *says* what she found, and when something breaks she says that too — a plain narrative, never a stack trace read aloud. The artifact layer is a distinct thing from Lilith, and the narrative layer is how she truthfully bridges the two without breaking the feel of conversation.

## Engineering at a high level

The build order inside the stretch matters: provider adapter first (everything needs an LLM to talk to), then personality files (cheap, and needed by the context manager), then the context manager with the tokenization pass (the hard middle), then the router (which needs both lanes to route between), then CH-04's schema, tool runner, and narratives. The seam to respect most is the streaming path — reverse-substitution happens *inside* the token stream on its way to the user, so the substitution filter must be built as a stream transformer from day one, not as a post-processor retrofitted later.

Testing posture: the PII layer gets adversarial tests (split tags, fabricated tags, tags at chunk boundaries, PII in the middle of code-switching sentences) against a mocked endpoint where we can inspect every outbound byte. The router gets representative-input tests but *not* accuracy tuning — that's CH-08's job, against a set that must be frozen before tuning begins. Memory gets round-trip tests: dictate, persist, recall, verify.

What we defer without guilt: the artifact side of the PII lifecycle (mapping snapshots, re-lock cancellation) is CH-10 scope by explicit amendment; local models are CH-11; every tool beyond `recall_memory` waits for its app.

## What done feels like

Two moments end this stretch. First: you type a sentence containing your friend's name and email, watch the outbound payload in the test harness, and see only anonymous tags where the personal facts were — then watch Lilith's streamed answer arrive with the real name back in place, and nothing in the database or logs any the wiser. The border crossing works.

Second: you dictate a note about your day through the text path, close the session, open a new one, and ask what you said. She routes it, says "let me check," finds it, and tells you — in her own voice, from her own memory, through her own tool. It's the first complete loop of *Lilith being useful*, and every later capability is this loop with more verbs. The next report gives her a face and puts the voice back on both ends.
