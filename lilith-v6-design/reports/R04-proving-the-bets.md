# Report 04 — Proving the Bets (CH-07, CH-08, EXP-3)

*Covers Chapter CH-07 (Experiment 1: Walking-Skeleton Latency Benchmark), Chapter CH-08 (Experiment 2: Router Dry-Run), and the floating gate EXP-3 (Encrypted Vector Feasibility). The law for this stretch is `../BUILD_SEQUENCING.md` §3, executed exactly as written. This report is about why we stop building to run experiments — and why we've pre-committed to consequences we might not like.*

---

## Where we are in the story

Milestone 1 works. Lilith listens, thinks, remembers, and answers, and the developer talks to her daily. Which makes this precisely the most dangerous moment of the whole project — the moment every previous era kept building.

v6's design phase did something unusual for a solo project: it went through three rounds of adversarial review, in which the reviewers' explicit job was to attack the design's assumptions. Two assumptions survived only by being converted from *beliefs* into *experiments with pre-committed pass bars and named fallback plans*. This stretch runs those experiments. No new features ship. The deliverable of both chapters is a report artifact and a decision — and we've promised, in writing, in advance, to obey the decision.

The thinking behind that promise is worth spelling out, because it's the intellectual backbone of the whole build: **a measurement you take after you've fallen in love with your architecture is not a measurement, it's a testimony.** The thresholds were committed on 2026-07-12 — before the utterance set existed, before a single router rule was written — so that no amount of affection for the two-mode fork can quietly move the goalposts. And the fallback for each failure is *named* — a specific alternative architecture with a design session attached — so that failing a gate produces a next step instead of a crisis.

## What we're testing, in plain English

**CH-07 puts a stopwatch on the conversation.** The core promise of the conversational lane is *under about three seconds from when you stop talking to when she starts talking* — on modest CPU-only hardware, through the real pipeline. And "real" is the operative word: an earlier draft of this benchmark would have measured a pipeline without the PII tokenization pass, then bolted the privacy layer on afterward and hoped. The design reviews caught that, and the amended experiment runs the full 50-query set twice — tokenization on and off — so we get both the honest number (with privacy active, judged against a 3.5-second p90 bar) and the isolated cost of privacy itself. The queries deliberately contain names, emails, and phone numbers, with expected responses that echo them back, so the substitution machinery actually works during the measurement instead of idling.

**CH-08 tries to kill the router.** The two-mode fork routes each utterance *before* generation using deterministic keyword rules. Two separate doubts attack this: can rules route accurately at all? And even if they can — is pre-classification actually better than the steelmanned alternative, a single always-streaming path that only promotes to the artifact lane when the output *demonstrably* needs it? The dry-run instruments a genuine counterfactual: 100 realistic utterances, frozen and hash-stamped *before* any rule tuning, each run through both the router's decision and a full uncapped generation, with a mechanical verdict per turn — no judgment calls, two people computing from the same log get the same answer. The pre-committed gates: more than 10% total misroutes, or more than 5% in the user-audible direction (a conversational routing that guillotines an answer mid-sentence or loses a needed tool), and we are *required* to hold the stream-and-cut design session before hardening anything more on top of the router.

**EXP-3 guards the memory system's future.** It isn't in the sequence — it floats, runnable any time before hybrid recall is adopted — but it belongs in this report because it's the same discipline aimed at a different bet: that a pre-v1 vector-search extension actually loads and performs *inside this project's specific SQLCipher build*, at the scales and latencies the recall roadmap needs. Upstream claims of compatibility were reviewed and rated unproven for our build. Until this experiment passes on our hardware with our pinned versions, not one embedding dependency enters the codebase, and Lilith's memory stays proudly on full-text search.

## What we'll be thinking about

**Failing a gate is a good outcome.** It needs saying plainly: if Experiment 1 blows the budget or Experiment 2 fires the fallback review, the experiments have *worked*. The catastrophic version of this project — the one the blog's history warns about — is the one where a foundational flaw is discovered in chapter twelve, load-bearing, with a year of code on top of it. The gates exist to force that discovery to happen now, in a codebase small enough to pivot. We will run them with genuine curiosity rather than defensiveness, and the reports will record what *is*, not what we hoped.

**The fallback ladders are part of the architecture.** Experiment 1 failing doesn't mean improvising: the ladder is written. First check whether the tokenization delta explains the overrun (optimize the detector, which is required to stay single-pass, before touching architecture). Then the stream-and-cut pattern. Then a soft token cap that lets her finish a sentence instead of truncating mid-word. Experiment 2 failing schedules a specific named design session with specific named remedies still on the table. Knowing the exits before entering the room is what makes it safe to test honestly.

**Measurement infrastructure is a deliverable, not scaffolding.** Both experiments force the creation of things with long lives: a latency harness that times the pipeline stage by stage, a frozen utterance corpus with a recorded hash, per-turn instrumentation events shaped to the telemetry taxonomy, JSONL result artifacts. These become the project's permanent benchmarking muscle — when CH-09 brings real telemetry and later chapters tune the numeric working hypotheses through dogfooding, they'll do it with the instruments built here. We'll build them as keepers, not throwaways.

**Statistical honesty at small scale.** A hundred utterances is a small N, and the design acknowledges it like an adult: the directional gate's noise note works out that if the true misroute rate were an acceptable ~2%, the probability of the gate firing anyway is under ~2%. We'll hold both attitudes at once — take the numbers seriously as decision inputs, and resist over-reading any single percentage point. The set is a stratified, deliberately hard set (boundary cases, tool-needing requests phrased innocently, questions conversational in form but long in answer), which means the measured rate is a stress-test rate, not a field rate. Dogfooding provides the field rate over time.

**No tuning against the test.** The most human failure mode available in this stretch: run the dry-run, see which utterances misroute, tweak the rules for exactly those, re-run, declare victory. The law explicitly separates expected engineering (tuning after a failed run) from self-deception (a re-run canceling an already-triggered fallback review — it can't), and the frozen hash exists so the set can't quietly evolve toward the rules. We'll treat the corpus like the evidence it is.

## Engineering at a high level

Both experiments are harness-writing jobs. CH-07 needs simulated-network conditions, precise stage timestamps (voice offset to first audio chunk), and clean A/B switching of the tokenization pass — which incidentally proves that layer is properly modular. CH-08 needs corpus tooling (author, freeze, hash), a dual-path runner (router decision + uncapped generation with provider-reported token counts), the mechanical verdict function, and the report generator. EXP-3, whenever it runs, is a compatibility-and-benchmark rig: pinned extension builds loaded into SQLCipher-keyed databases, synthetic vector tables at three scales, timed KNN with a concurrent FTS5 query, all recorded with hardware specs and versions.

The order between CH-07 and CH-08 is free — the guide allows either first — and the natural choice is whichever the dogfooding experience makes more urgent: if daily use already *feels* slow, run the stopwatch first; if routing already *feels* wrong, run the dry-run first. Lived experience gets a vote in scheduling, never in verdicts.

## What done feels like

Two documents exist that didn't before, and they are the most consequential artifacts of the project so far: reports with real numbers, hash-stamped inputs, and one of two clearly-written outcomes each. Best case, both gates close green — the ≤3-second promise is measured truth with privacy included, the two-mode fork survives its own execution attempt, and every chapter after this builds on *validated* ground with the fallback forks formally closed for v1. Worst case, one or both fire — and we walk into a design session with data in hand, named options on the table, and a codebase still small enough to change its mind.

Either way, something rare has happened for a personal project: the architecture stopped being a story we tell ourselves and became a claim we tested. The next report is about hardening what survived.
