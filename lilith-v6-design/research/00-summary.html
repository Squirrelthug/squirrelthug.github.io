If you read only this page: A great personal assistant is not an artificial friend and not an omniscient oracle. Across 4,000 years of human history and 30 years of HCI research, great assistants are bounded cognitive prostheses and protective filters that earn trust through flawless reliability on narrow tasks first, maintain strict boundary discipline, make their memory and actions inspectable and reversible, and challenge flawed assumptions rather than flattering their principal. Moreover, five assumptions in Lilith's current brief—naive name/number tokenisation, silent specialist-mode isolation, invisible local/cloud switching, self-readable telemetry, and companion-style framing—conflict with empirical evidence and must be resolved before writing code.

1. Reliability and Boundary Discipline Beat Anticipation ([evidence | confidence: high])

  • The Finding: Across 11 historical assistant professions and empirical trust models, anticipation is a late-stage optimization, not a baseline behaviour. In early interactions, trustworthiness is governed by Ability (flawless execution of narrow promises) and Integrity (knowing the limits of one's authority and saying "I can't do that yet" cleanly), whereas premature anticipation without deep context produces destructive commission errors (" not checked: source could not be openedThe Sorcerer's Apprentice Syndrome").
  • Evidence & Pointer: Mayer, Davis, & Schoorman (1995) (https://doi.org/10.5465/amr.1995.9508080335) S60; Luger & Sellen (2016) ("Like Having a Really Bad PA", https://doi.org/10.1145/2858036.2858288) S81; Bainbridge (1983) (https://doi.org/10.1016/0005-1098(83)90046-8) S124. See: [01-historical-roles.md]01-historical-roles.md & [02-principles.md]02-principles.md (Principles #1–#4 vs. #11).

2. Tokenising Names and Numbers Fails Privacy and Breaks Reasoning ([evidence | confidence: high])

  • The Finding: Your brief's plan to tokenise names and numbers before calling a cloud model is scientifically flawed. Transformer NER scrubbers miss 3% of names and 20% of unit numbers (Lukas et al., 2023), 87% of Americans are uniquely identified by {ZIP, gender, DOB} without names (Sweeney, 2000), and GPT-4 infers protected attributes (city, age, sex, occupation) from free text at 85.5% accuracy—and still achieves 43.9%–64.2% accuracy even after Azure NER strips all names, numbers, dates, locations, orgs, and relational nouns (Staab et al., 2024). Conversely, replacing names with [PERSON_1] strips the relational context (CEO vs. spouse vs. oncologist) the cloud model needs to reason. Personal and third-party bystander data must either stay 100% local or pass through a structural rewrite gateway with user preview.
  • Evidence & Pointer: Staab et al. (2024) (ICLR 2024, https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.07298) S147; Lukas et al. (2023) (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.00539) S134; Sweeney (2000) (https://dataprivacylab.org/projects/identifiability/paper1.pdf) S149; Narayanan & Shmatikov (2008) (https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0610105) S137. See: [08-questions-you-didnt-ask.md]08-questions-you-didnt-ask.md (§8.1) & [09-implications-for-lilith.md]09-implications-for-lilith.md (Conflict C1, Hypothesis H1).

3. Never Front-Load a "Getting-to-Know-You" Interview on Day 1 ([evidence | confidence: high])

  • The Finding: Unlike a human executive who has high sunk cost in a new hire, software users have zero tolerance for upfront onboarding questionnaires before receiving value (Mackay, 1991; Shavit et al., 2023: "users don’t want to have to tell their life story just to get help baking a cake"). Upfront self-reports also capture espoused theory rather than actual theory-in-use (Argyris & Schön, 1974), and proactive personal questioning mimics a privacy-profiling adversary (Staab et al., 2024). Onboarding must be task-first under conservative defaults, asking at most one post-task preference check-in question and storing rules in an inspectable, plain-text Working Agreement.
  • Evidence & Pointer: Shavit et al. (2023) (https://cdn.openai.com/papers/practices-for-governing-agentic-ai-systems.pdf) S145; Mackay (1991) (https://doi.org/10.1145/108844.108867) S121; Argyris & Schön (1974) S36; Kim et al. (2004) (https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.89.1.104) S66. See: [03-trust-and-onboarding.md]03-trust-and-onboarding.md (§3.4–§3.5).

4. Help Users Find Gaps via Reference-Interview Verification & Closed Options ([evidence | confidence: high])

  • The Finding: People ask assistants compromised queries—phrasing requests in terms of what they think the system can do rather than what they actually need (Taylor, 1968). To help users find gaps without interrogating them, use the librarian Reference Interview pattern: restate the inferred goal and offer 2–3 closed, concrete interpretations rather than open-ended questions, and discover adjacent tools via Post-Completion Adjacency Footers ("I did X; next time I can also do Y"), capped at <10% of turns with a 30-day dismissal cooldown.
  • Evidence & Pointer: Taylor (1968) (Question-Negotiation, https://doi.org/10.5860/crl_29_03_178) S15; RUSA Guidelines (2013) (https://www.ala.org/rusa/resources/guidelines/guidelinesbehavioral) S45; Horvitz (1999) (https://erichorvitz.com/chi99horvitz.pdf) S73. See: [07-capability-awareness-and-handoff.md]07-capability-awareness-and-handoff.md (§7.1–§7.2).

5. Keep One Front-Stage Persona, Add Perceptual Mode Feedback, and Sandbox the Nexus ([evidence | confidence: high])

  • The Finding: Controlled HCI experiments prove users strongly prefer one continuous front-stage persona that loads specialist tools over being handed off to separate specialist personas (Clarke et al., 2022/2024). However, your claim that "specialisation changes her behaviour in that specialist mode only, not her everyday interactions" not checked: source could not be opened ignores two hazards: (1) human mode errors when one persona changes rules without continuous visual/audio feedback (Norman, 1983; Sellen et al., 1992), and (2) Simon Willison's "Lethal Trifecta" (Willison, 2025; Greshake et al., 2023), where loading external Nexus harnesses/content alongside private notes/contacts and outbound tools enables trivial indirect prompt-injection data theft. Nexus harnesses must run in air-gapped sandboxes that export deadlines/notes back to everyday Lilith only via user-approved Handoff Cards (SBAR/I-PASS).
  • Evidence & Pointer: Clarke et al. (2022/2024) (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.07348.pdf) S79; Sellen et al. (1992) (https://doi.org/10.1207/s15327051hci0702_1) S139; Willison (2025) (https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jun/16/the-lethal-trifecta/) S152; Greshake et al. (2023) (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.12173) S131; Starmer et al. (2014) (https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMsa1405556) S55. See: [07-capability-awareness-and-handoff.md]07-capability-awareness-and-handoff.md (§7.3) & [08-questions-you-didnt-ask.md]08-questions-you-didnt-ask.md (§8.2, §8.4).

6. Replace Confirmation Spam With a 3-Tier Reversibility Matrix & Action Ledger ([evidence | confidence: high])

  • The Finding: Asking permission before every write action backfires catastrophically: across 1.59 million clinical alerts, every additional alert drops acceptance by 30% ($OR = 0.70$), and repeat alerts are overridden 87.9%–99.9% of the time (Ancker et al., 2017). Instead, classify actions by reversibility and blast radius: execute Tier-0 (reads/drafts) and Tier-1 (local reversible note/calendar edits) optimistically with a 1-click Undo Toast and immutable Action Ledger (Shavit et al., 2023), reserving interruptive, parameter-explicit Outbox confirmations strictly for Tier-2 irreversible or external/third-party actions (Ruan et al., 2024 ToolEmu).
  • Evidence & Pointer: Ancker et al. (2017) (https://doi.org/10.1186/s12911-017-0430-8) S123; Ruan et al. (2023/2024) (https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2309.15817) S141; Shavit et al. (2023) S145. See: [06-pitfalls.md]06-pitfalls.md (§6.6, §6.8) & [09-implications-for-lilith.md]09-implications-for-lilith.md (Hypothesis H5).

7. Make Memory Transparent, Provenance-Linked, and Built for "Forgetting" ([evidence | confidence: high])

  • The Finding: Black-box LLM memory creates severe privacy and trust hazards: 82.7% of ChatGPT users are unaware hidden memory is active while it extracts sensitive diagnoses and ages (Zaman & Hoque, 2026), and query-only attacks (MINJA) poison agent memory at a 98.2% success rate (Dong et al., 2025). Furthermore, when users go through breakups, bereavement, or job changes, they need selective unlearning (Sas & Whittaker, 2013). Every memory, summary, and embedding must store foreign-key provenance lineage (derived_from_record_ids) so deleting a note or archiving a life chapter cascades cleanly without leaving "summary ghosts."
  • Evidence & Pointer: Zaman & Hoque (2026) (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.14697v1.pdf) S95; Dong et al. (2025) (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.03704) S127; Sas & Whittaker (2013) (https://doi.org/10.1145/2470654.2466241) S142. See: [04-digital-assistants-evidence.md]04-digital-assistants-evidence.md (§4.6), [06-pitfalls.md]06-pitfalls.md (§6.7), & [08-questions-you-didnt-ask.md]08-questions-you-didnt-ask.md (§8.9).

8. Adapt via Lexical Mirroring, Scaffold Fading, and a Simple/Full Toggle—Never "Elderspeak" ([evidence | confidence: high])

  • The Finding: Do not use a black-box classifier to "dumb down" the persona for less technical users—over-accommodation triggers the patronizing communication / elderspeak predicament (Ryan et al., 1995; Williams et al., 2004), while over-explaining to experts triggers the Expertise Reversal Effect (Kalyuga et al., 2003). Instead, adapt implicitly by mirroring the user's lexical specificity in-turn (Isaacs & Clark, 1987), hold respectful adult dignity constant, offer a user-controlled Personal (Simple) vs. Full Workspace toggle (McGrenere et al., 2002), and build in W3C COGA and screen-reader/dysarthric speech accommodations (Pradhan et al., 2018; Branham & Roy, 2019).
  • Evidence & Pointer: Isaacs & Clark (1987) (https://web.stanford.edu/~clark/1980s/Isaacs,%20E.A.%20_%20Clark,%20H.H.%20_References%20in%20conversations%20between%20experts%20and%20novices_%201987.pdf) S107; Williams et al. (2004) S110; Kalyuga et al. (2003) S112; McGrenere et al. (2002) (https://www.cs.ubc.ca/labs/edapt/papers/mcGrenere2002.pdf) S120; Pradhan et al. (2018) S115. See: [05-adaptation-and-accessibility.md]05-adaptation-and-accessibility.md.

9. Defend Against Sycophancy and Deskilling With Blinded Critique & Stewardship Framing ([evidence | confidence: high])

  • The Finding: Great human Chiefs of Staff practice Constructive Challenge (Principle 13 in [02-principles.md]02-principles.md), whereas RLHF-trained LLMs switch from truthful to sycophantic answers 14.5%–27% of the time when a user states an opinion (Sharma et al., 2024), eroding knowledge workers' critical thinking (Lee et al., 2025, $\beta = -0.29, p = 0.004$). When asked to review a user's draft or plan, Lilith should run an internal blinded critic pass (evaluating the text without the user's stance attached) and frame the user as the task steward.
  • Evidence & Pointer: Sharma et al. (2023/2024) (ICLR 2024, https://arxiv.org/html/2310.13548v4) S144; Lee et al. (2025) (CHI '25, https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/lee_2025_ai_critical_thinking_survey.pdf) S130; Ciampa (2020) S13. See: [02-principles.md]02-principles.md (§2.2 Principle #13) & [06-pitfalls.md]06-pitfalls.md (§6.2–§6.3).

10. Draw a Hard Line Between "Professional Staff" and "Emotional Companion" ([evidence | confidence: high])

  • The Finding: Naming a voice-capable assistant "Lilith" and aiming to be "there for the user" invites parasocial attachment, intimate over-sharing, and reliance during mental health crises (Gabriel et al., 2024; Laestadius et al., 2022)—where frontier LLMs validate delusional beliefs ~55% of the time and fail acute crisis safety checks >20% of the time (Moore et al., 2025). Lilith requires a strict Anti-Companion Charter (no simulated affection or loneliness cues) and a deterministic pre-LLM crisis circuit-breaker that blocks lethal-means queries, halts memory writes during distress turns, and hands off to human crisis resources.
  • Evidence & Pointer: Gabriel et al. (2024) (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.16244) S128; Moore et al. (2025) (ACM FAccT '25, https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18412v1) S135; Laestadius et al. (2022/2024) (https://doi.org/10.1177/14614448221142007) S90. See: [06-pitfalls.md]06-pitfalls.md (§6.1, §6.11) & [08-questions-you-didnt-ask.md]08-questions-you-didnt-ask.md (§8.10).
