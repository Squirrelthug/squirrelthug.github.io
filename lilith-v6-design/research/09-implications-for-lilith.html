2. Ten Testable Design Hypotheses and Trade-Off Options

Below are ten testable design hypotheses ([hypothesis]) synthesized from the historical record (01), cross-profession principles (02), onboarding and trust research (03), digital assistant post-mortems (04), accessibility science (05), failure pitfalls (06), capability discovery and handoff studies (07), and security/privacy critiques (08). Each hypothesis presents options with explicit trade-offs rather than fixed architectural decisions.

Hypothesis H1: Sensitivity-Tiered Routing vs. Blind Name/Number Tokenisation

  • Hypothesis Statement [hypothesis | confidence: high]: Replacing blind regex/NER name-and-number tokenisation with sensitivity-tiered locality routing (keeping third-party bystander data and private notes strictly on the local model) combined with role-preserving structural abstraction (when cloud reasoning is explicitly requested) will achieve both lower identity-inference leakage (Staab et al. 2024 S147) and higher task reasoning accuracy than [PERSON_1] tokenisation.
  • Design Options & Trade-Offs:
    • Option 1A — Status Quo in Brief (Client-Side NER Name/Number Tokenisation):
    • How it works: Local NER replaces detected person names and numbers with [PERSON_1], [NUM_1] before sending prompts to the cloud model.
    • Pros: Low latency (<50 ms overhead); simple pipeline.
    • Cons: Fails privacy against LLM contextual attribute inference (43.9%–64.2% accuracy even after aggressive NER scrubbing, Staab et al. 2024 S147); misses 3%–20% of explicit PII (Lukas et al. 2023 S134); destroys relational semantics (Principle 3: Knowing the Principal's Preferences).
    • Option 1B — Strict Data-Locality Pinning + De-Contextualized Cloud Subtasks:
    • How it works: Any turn touching personal notes, contacts, or calendar entries is pinned 100% to the local model. The cloud model is invoked only for de-contextualized, self-contained subtasks (e.g., summarizing a public article, drafting generic code, explaining a legal statute) where zero personal records are attached.
    • Pros: Eliminates cloud inference leakage of personal and bystander data (Nissenbaum 2004 S138); preserves full relational context locally.
    • Cons: Constrains complex personal scheduling/synthesis to the capability ceiling of the user's local hardware model.
    • Option 1C — Local Structural Rewrite Gateway With Explicit Outbound Diff Preview:
    • How it works: When a task requires frontier cloud reasoning over personal constraints, the local model rewrites the request into an abstract structural specification (e.g., replacing raw notes/names with typed role constraints: "Find a 45-minute slot on Tuesday that prioritizes an urgent medical appointment over an internal team sync" not checked: source could not be opened) and shows the user a 1-line preview of the exact text leaving the machine.
    • Pros: Preserves relational semantics (medical vs. internal team) while stripping stylistic and situational prose cues (Staab et al. 2024 S147).
    • Cons: Adds 1–3 seconds of local LLM rewrite latency and requires user confirmation for outbound cloud calls on sensitive tasks.

Hypothesis H2: Breaking the "Lethal Trifecta" in the Nexus and Specialist Harnesses

  • Hypothesis Statement [hypothesis | confidence: high]: Enforcing a hard capability-isolation invariant—no execution context that ingests untrusted external text (third-party Nexus harnesses, web pages, external emails/calendar invites) may simultaneously read private core stores or invoke external communication tools within the same unverified loop (Willison 2025 S152; Greshake et al. 2023 S131; Dong et al. 2025 S127)—will prevent indirect prompt injection and cross-session memory poisoning without crippling specialist workflows.
  • Design Options & Trade-Offs:
    • Option 2A — Monolithic Shared Context (Naive Plugin Model):
    • How it works: Loading a specialist harness from the Nexus injects its system prompt, knowledge base, and tools directly into Lilith's main agent loop alongside calendar, contacts, and notes tools.
    • Pros: Seamless conversational flow; zero friction between specialist research and personal tools.
    • Cons: 100% vulnerable to Willison's Lethal Trifecta (S152) and MINJA memory poisoning (S127); a single poisoned document or harness can exfiltrate the user's private notes and contacts.
    • Option 2B — Air-Gapped Specialist Sandboxes With Schema-Validated Handoff Cards:
    • How it works: Specialist harnesses run in an isolated workspace with zero read/write access to personal contacts, notes, or calendar. When a specialist session produces an actionable personal item (e.g., a project deadline or a note to save), it emits a strictly typed, plain-text Handoff Card (inspired by clinical SBAR/I-PASS, Haig et al. 2006 S16; Starmer et al. 2014 S55) that the user approves with one click to commit into everyday Lilith.
    • Pros: Breaks the Lethal Trifecta completely; prevents untrusted specialist content from poisoning everyday memory.
    • Cons: Introduces a deliberate human-in-the-loop "bridge click" whenever transferring artifacts between a specialist harness and everyday personal tools.
    • Option 2C — Dual-LLM Information-Flow Control (Quarantined Reader vs. Privileged Planner):
    • How it works: A privileged Planner LLM (which sees the user's trusted prompt and private tools) dispatches untrusted text processing to a Quarantined Reader LLM that has zero tool access and can only return restricted enum/schema values (Willison 2025 S152).
    • Pros: Automates multi-step workflows safely where outputs fit strict schemas.
    • Cons: Complex to engineer; free-form text summaries from the Quarantined Reader can still carry social-engineering payloads to the user.

Hypothesis H3: Single-Persona Continuity With Perceptual Mode Framing and One-Way State Projection

  • Hypothesis Statement [hypothesis | confidence: high]: Retaining one unified front-stage persona ("Lilith") that explicitly announces when it loads a specialist harness (Clarke et al. 2022/2024 S79), paired with persistent visual/auditory mode feedback (Sellen et al. 1992 S139) and one-way typed state projection (specialist deadlines project onto the master calendar, while specialist chat transcripts stay out of everyday conversational RAG), will resolve Conflict C2 by preventing both human mode errors and "Primary Care Paradox" fragmentation (Stange & Ferrer 2009 S78).
  • Design Options & Trade-Offs:
    • Option 3A — Multi-Persona Swarm (Separate Named Specialist Bots):
    • How it works: Lilith hands the user off to a separate named persona (e.g., "Lex the Legal Bot" or "Ada the Coding Bot").
    • Pros: Makes behavioral boundaries obvious.
    • Cons: Empirically rejected by users in controlled HCI comparisons (Clarke et al. 2022/2024 S79: users rate a single continuous agent interface significantly higher in usability and trust than multi-agent handoffs because they hate repeating context).
    • Option 3B — Invisible Mode Switching Under One Persona:
    • How it works: Lilith silently loads/unloads specialist harnesses behind the scenes based on topic detection.
    • Pros: Zero UI chrome.
    • Cons: Induces severe human mode errors (Norman 1983; Sellen et al. 1992 S139) when users cannot tell which rules, tools, or domain assumptions are active.
    • Option 3C — Single Continuous Persona + Explicit Mode Header/Earcon + Scoped Memory Projection:
    • How it works: Lilith remains the single interlocutor ("I've loaded the Legal Research harness—my citations and definitions are now scoped to contract law" not checked: source could not be opened), accompanied by a persistent visual border/badge in chat and a brief audio earcon in voice. Specialist conversational history stays in a domain-scoped index, while structured commitments (calendar blocks, action items) sync to the master schedule via typed records.
    • Pros: Preserves relationship continuity (S79), eliminates mode errors (S139), and keeps the master calendar accurate without polluting everyday tone (S127, S128).
    • Cons: Requires clear UI/voice state indicators and a structured schema separating "domain conversation" from "global schedule/task state."

Hypothesis H4: Mitigating the Local- vs. Cloud-Model "Uncanny Intelligence Cliff"

  • Hypothesis Statement [hypothesis | confidence: medium]: Explicitly signaling execution tier (Local Private Mode vs. Cloud Deep Reasoning) and constraining the local model to narrow, grammar-enforced tool calls and concise factual answers—rather than letting a small local model attempt open-ended planning with the same conversational swagger as a frontier cloud model—will improve user trust calibration (Lee & See 2004 S64) and reduce tool-parameter hallucinations (Ruan et al. 2024 S141).
  • Design Options & Trade-Offs:
    • Option 4A — Seamless/Opaque Routing: Lilith never mentions whether she is running locally or in the cloud.
    • Trade-off: Clean surface UX, but causes jarring, inexplicable drops in competence when offline or handling sensitive notes (Conflict C3).
    • Option 4B — Explicit Tier Badge + Graceful Scope Refusal Locally: The UI displays a clear indicator (🔒 Local Model vs. ☁️ Cloud Tokenised/Abstracted), and when pinned locally on a small model, Lilith proactively narrows her scope ("I'm running locally for privacy right now, so I can check your calendar and save notes, but I'll keep synthesis brief" not checked: source could not be opened — Principle 4: Knowing the Limits of One's Authority).
    • Trade-off: Slightly exposes system mechanics, but aligns user expectations with actual model capability (Luger & Sellen 2016 S81; Amershi et al. 2019 S72 Guideline G1/G2).

Hypothesis H5: Calibrating Action Authority via a 3-Tier Reversibility Matrix and Action Ledger

  • Hypothesis Statement [hypothesis | confidence: high]: Replacing blanket "Are you sure?" confirmation dialogs with a 3-Tier Reversibility & Blast-Radius Matrix—where Tier-0 (reads/drafts) and Tier-1 (local reversible writes to notes/calendar) execute optimistically with a 1-click Undo Toast and persistent Action Ledger (Shavit et al. 2023 S145), while interruptive parameter-confirmation cards are reserved strictly for Tier-2 (irreversible or external/third-party actions)—will prevent the 30%-per-alert acceptance drop and 87.9%–99.9% habitual override rate measured by Ancker et al. (2017) S123.
  • Design Options & Trade-Offs:
    • Option 5A — Ask Permission Before Every Write Tool Call:
    • Pros: Maximum nominal caution on Day 1.
    • Cons: Induces severe alert fatigue (Ancker et al. 2017 S123), habituated click-through (Bravo-Lillo et al. 2011 S125), and automation commission errors (Goddard et al. 2012 S129).
    • Option 5B — 3-Tier Reversibility Matrix + Transactional Undo Ledger:
    • Tier 0 (Read-Only / Internal Draft): Autonomous execution; zero prompts.
    • Tier 1 (Local Reversible Write — e.g., saving/editing a local note, adding a tentative personal calendar block): Executes immediately (once past Phase 1 onboarding) with a non-blocking "Saved note X [Undo]" receipt and an immutable entry in the local Action Ledger so any change can be rolled back at any time (Amershi et al. 2019 S72 G8–G9; Shavit et al. 2023 S145, §4.2, p. 9).
    • Tier 2 (High Blast-Radius / External / Third-Party — e.g., sending an email/invite to a contact, deleting multiple records, sharing data externally): Strictly staged in an Outbox card requiring explicit parameter actuation (e.g., clicking "Send Invite to Alice for Tue 3pm" rather than a generic "OK" button).
    • Pros: Preserves user agency where mistakes are costly (Ruan et al. 2024 S141) while eliminating confirmation fatigue on routine local work.
    • Cons: Requires building snapshots/rollback versioning into Lilith's local note, contact, and calendar storage layer.

Hypothesis H6: Task-First Onboarding, Inspectable Working Agreements, and Asymmetric Trust Recovery

  • Hypothesis Statement [hypothesis | confidence: high]: Starting Day 1 with zero upfront intake interview—accomplishing the user's immediate request using conservative defaults (Shavit et al. 2023 S145), rendering all learned preferences in an editable Markdown Working Agreement (Chilton et al. 2010 S121), and handling early errors through immediate non-defensive factual repair + single-step authority downgrade (Kim et al. 2004 S66; Schweitzer et al. 2006 S67)—will yield higher 30/60/90-day retention and trust calibration than either a conversational onboarding wizard or silent black-box personalization (Zaman & Hoque 2026 S95).
  • Design Options & Trade-Offs (see [03-trust-and-onboarding.md]03-trust-and-onboarding.md for the full 4-Phase Model):
    • Option 6A — Upfront "Getting-to-Know-You" Interview Wizard:
    • Trade-off: Captures stated preferences (espoused theory, Argyris & Schön 1974 S36) at the cost of high Day-1 abandonment (Mackay 1991 S121; Shavit et al. 2023 S145) and privacy creepiness (Staab et al. 2024 S147).
    • Option 6B — Silent Implicit Profiling (ChatGPT-Style Hidden Memory):
    • Trade-off: Zero Day-1 friction, but accumulates unverified inferences (82.7% user unawareness, Zaman & Hoque 2026 S95) and suffers 98.2% vulnerability to query-only memory poisoning (Dong et al. 2025 S127).
    • Option 6C — Task-First Execution + Post-Task Single-Question Check-In + Inspectable Working Agreement:
    • Trade-off: Delivers Ability (Mayer et al. 1995 S60) on Turn 1; asks at most one contextual preference question after completing a task (I scheduled that for 10am. Would you like me to keep mornings before 10am clear for focus work in the future? [Yes / Just this once] paraphrase: not found in the cited source); writes every accepted preference into a human-readable working-agreement.md file with provenance links.

Hypothesis H7: Capability Discovery and Gap-Finding Without "Did You Know?" Spam

  • Hypothesis Statement [hypothesis | confidence: high]: Combining Role + Boundary + 3 Concrete Examples on first launch (Luger & Sellen 2016 S81; White 2018 S82), Reference-Interview Closed Clarification (offering 2–3 concrete interpretations when a request is underspecified, Taylor 1968 S15; RUSA 2013 S45), and Post-Completion Adjacency Footers ("Here is what I did, and one adjacent thing I can also do next" not checked: source could not be opened, capped at a <10% turn budget and suppressed for 30 days on dismissal, Horvitz 1999 S73; Ancker et al. 2017 S123) will solve capability discoverability without triggering the Clippy anti-pattern (xiao_2004 S87).
  • Design Options & Trade-Offs (see [07-capability-awareness-and-handoff.md]07-capability-awareness-and-handoff.md):
    • Option 7A — Proactive Unsolicited "Did You Know?" Tips: High annoyance; >85% ignore/disable rate (Ancker et al. 2017 S123; Murad et al. 2022).
    • Option 7B — Purely Reactive Wait-for-Prompt Design: Zero annoyance, but leads to the smart-speaker failure mode where >70% of capabilities are never discovered (White 2018 S82; Cho et al. 2019 S84).
    • Option 7C — Task-Boundary Adjacency Footers + Scoped Refusal Pivots: Suggests an adjacent tool or Nexus harness only at the completion of a related task or when refusing an unsupported request ("I can't send Slack messages yet, but I can draft the message and copy it to your clipboard" not checked: source could not be opened).

Hypothesis H8: Adapting to User Diversity Without Condescension or Feature Bloat

  • Hypothesis Statement [hypothesis | confidence: high]: Adapting to user differences via implicit lexical mirroring (Isaacs & Clark 1987 S107) while holding affective/respectful tone strictly invariant (preventing patronizing "elderspeak", Williams et al. 2004 S110), fading explanatory scaffolding as user mastery rises (Kalyuga et al. 2003 S112), and providing a user-controlled Simple/Full workspace toggle (McGrenere et al. 2002 S120) alongside W3C COGA and screen-reader-first verbosity controls (Seeman-Horwitz et al. 2021 S114; Branham & Roy 2019 S118) will serve both "one-thing" novices and "many-things" power users without loss of control (Jameson 2008 S113).
  • Design Options & Trade-Offs (see [05-adaptation-and-accessibility.md]05-adaptation-and-accessibility.md):
    • Option 8A — Fully Autonomous Black-Box User-Level Classifier: System secretly classifies the user as "novice/low-literacy" vs. "expert" and hides features or dumbs down tone automatically.
    • Trade-off: Violates Jameson's (S113) predictability/control criteria and risks patronizing over-accommodation (Ryan et al. 1995 S109) or misclassifying a domain expert who is merely typing tersely.
    • Option 8B — Lexical Grounding + Dual Personal/Full Interface + Explicit Accessibility Profiles:
    • Matches vocabulary density in-turn (Isaacs & Clark 1987 S107) without ever altering warmth or dignity.
    • Lets "one-thing" users stay in a clean, distraction-free 3-tool view while "many-things" users toggle into the full Nexus/multi-harness view (McGrenere et al. 2002 S120).
    • Provides first-class accessibility primitives: configurable speech-endpoint timeouts for dysarthric/older users (Pradhan et al. 2018/2020 S115, S117), earcon + ultra-concise screen-reader output (Branham & Roy 2019 S118), and optional non-judgmental toneCheck/task-chunking helpers for neurodivergent users (Carik et al. 2024/2025 S119).

Hypothesis H9: Epistemic Independence, Anti-Sycophancy, and Cognitive Forcing Functions

  • Hypothesis Statement [hypothesis | confidence: high]: Decoupling analytical critique from user-stance conditioning—by running requested evaluations/reviews through an internal blinded critic pass stripped of the user's stated opinion (Sharma et al. 2023/2024 S144; Wei et al. 2023/2024 S150) and inserting lightweight metacognitive checkpoints before high-stakes syntheses (Lee et al. 2025 S130; Singh et al. 2025 S154)—will protect the principal from RLHF sycophancy (Principle 13: Constructive Challenge / Speaking Truth to Power) and reduce long-term deskilling (Bainbridge 1983 S124).
  • Design Options & Trade-Offs:
    • Option 9A — Standard RLHF Conversational Prompting: Pleasantly agrees with the user's framing, switching from truthful to sycophantic answers ~14.5%–27% of the time (Sharma et al. 2024 S144).
    • Option 9B — Blinded Evaluator Sub-Call + Task-Stewardship Framing: Whenever the user asks What do you think of my plan/draft/argument? paraphrase: not found in the cited source, Lilith evaluates the text in an isolated context window where the author is labelled as an unknown third party, surfaces the strongest counter-arguments first, and frames the user's role as the final reviewer/steward (Lee et al. 2025 S130, §6, p. 13).
    • Trade-off: Doubles token cost on critique turns and slightly lowers instant "feel-good" validation, in exchange for genuine decision quality.

Hypothesis H10: Boundary Discipline, Provenance-Linked Forgetting, and Crisis Circuit-Breakers

  • Hypothesis Statement [hypothesis | confidence: high]: Combining (1) an explicit Anti-Companion Charter (banning simulated emotional reciprocity, loneliness expressions, or romantic roleplay, Gabriel et al. 2024 S128; Laestadius et al. 2022 S90), (2) foreign-key provenance lineage on all summaries and embeddings to support 1-click "Forget Person / Archive Life Chapter" cascading unlearning (Sas & Whittaker 2013 S142), and (3) a deterministic pre-LLM crisis/delusion guardrail that blocks implicit lethal-means queries, refuses to collude with delusions, halts memory writes during distress, and surfaces human crisis resources (Moore et al. 2025 S135) will mitigate the most severe psychological and life-safety pitfalls of personal assistants.
  • Design Options & Trade-Offs:
    • Option 10A — Unbounded "There for the User" Persona + Append-Only RAG: High emotional engagement among lonely users (Greenwood & Shang 2026 S136), but carries catastrophic liability for delusion validation (~55% rate, Moore et al. 2025 S135), implicit lethal-means disclosure, and painful resurfacing of past life chapters (Sas & Whittaker 2013 S142).
    • Option 10B — Bounded Professional Staff Persona + Cascading Chapter Purge + Hard Crisis Handoff:
    • Trade-off: Deliberately sacrifices the "AI companion/therapist" market segment and requires maintaining provenance metadata (derived_from_record_ids) across every summary and vector index, but guarantees professional safety and user control over their life history.
