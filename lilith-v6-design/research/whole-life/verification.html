What was checked. Every URL in the sources list was fetched by script, and every passage in quotation marks on the pages was compared with the text of the sources cited beside it (and, failing that, with every readable source).

Not checked: the claims. Only links and quotations were tested, not whether a finding is true or whether a source supports a claim paraphrased from it. The research agents themselves could only read most pages as digests or abstracts.

Links

164 URLs in 123 sources were fetched. 120 were readable and checked; 11 opened but gave too little text to check a quotation against (landing pages, abstracts); 26 were blocked to scripts (login walls, firewalls, 403), which does not mean they are wrong; 7 were dead or unreachable.

Quotations

Of 106 quoted passages: 35 confirmed in the cited source, 8 found in a different readable source than the one cited, 49 not checkable (the cited source could not be read by a script), and 14 not found in a source that could be read. The not-found passages are shown on the pages as paraphrase. A few quoted phrases are the agents' own wording or the project's own terms and are left unflagged.

What to do with this

Treat each page as leads. Where a design decision will rest on a claim, open its source first.
