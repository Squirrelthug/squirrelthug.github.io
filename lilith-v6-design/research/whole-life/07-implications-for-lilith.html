How far to trust this page. This is AI research agents' synthesis, and most sources were read only as abstracts or summaries. No study was found comparing one integrated assistant with separate apps, so the value of joining everything up is a hypothesis. Quotations were checked by script: those marked not checked could not be tested, and those marked paraphrase were not found in the source they were credited to. Treat the structure and hypotheses as leads and open a source before relying on any claim. What was checked and what failed.

The design hypotheses and design-session questions from the five tracks, merged, de-duplicated and grouped by theme. Nothing here is a decision. Each item gives options and trade-offs and points to its evidence (page and finding, for example "03 F13"). Conflicts with the hard constraints are flagged with [CONFLICT] and the constraint named: - privacy: encrypted at rest, names and numbers tokenised before any cloud model, local model where the machine allows; - local-first; - narrow tools; - honest "can't".

Wave 1 already proposed several related mechanisms (a working agreement, a reversibility matrix, sandboxed harnesses with handoff cards, provenance lineage for memory; see wave 1 00-summary.md items 3, 5, 6, 7). Where a wave 2 question touches one of these, the wave 1 item is named rather than repeated.

Theme 1 - Whose assistant, and what role

1.1 Single principal or household? (01 design question 1, Q1, F13, Q9; 02 persona 2.7; 05 F16; 06 section 3.6) - (a) Strict single principal, with explicit records about other people. Simplest and safest; ignores the shared load the mental-load research is about. - (b) Shared household space with per-person scoping. Matches how lives work; brings coercion and consent risk and a harder permissions model. - Either way: assume an abusive or controlling co-user can exist; plan lock, recovery and hidden-domain behaviour (01 Q9). - [CONFLICT: privacy] (b) strains the single encrypted local database and per-domain consent unless carefully scoped. Track E's legal analysis assumed a single user (05 F16); how a shared household fits was not researched.

1.2 Which stage of cognitive labour does she own? (01 F3, design question 3; 02 F3; 03 F17, Q10) - (a) Anticipate, identify options and monitor; the human decides. Matches all tracks and wave 1's authority principle. - (b) Also decide and act on low-stakes items. More real relief; more error cost and trust risk. - Hypothesis to test: success measured as "things the user no longer has to remember", not "tasks completed" (01 Q2).

1.3 Scope line for professional records about third parties (02 Q5, design question 6; 01 design question 6) - Options: refuse professional records (patients, employees' performance) entirely; allow them only through a harness with its own consent and legal footing; allow only de-identified personal notes. - Trade-off: refusing disappoints the nurse, the carer and the manager; allowing puts regulated data in an unregulated product. - [CONFLICT: privacy] Clinical and HR records about others are regulated data (05 F17, F18). Also conflicts with a loose reading of "single assistant for the whole life".

1.4 Third-party health data in caregiving (01 F7, design question 6; 05 F17, Q2) - Options: allow; restrict; require a separate harness with extra protections; never send it to a cloud model unless the user explicitly chooses. - [CONFLICT: privacy] Tokenisation does not make health data non-personal (05 F15). Local-model-only handling degrades answers on low-end hardware (05 F23).

Theme 2 - Getting the life in: capture and integrations

2.1 Order of ingestion (01 design question 2, Q3, Q8, Q10; 02 Q11, design question 7; 05 DQ7, F19, F20, F25) - (a) Capture first: voice notes, photos of receipts and letters, typed one-liners. Works with no accounts and fits the plumber and farmer; needs reliable local transcription and OCR (capture accuracy is unresearched, 06 section 5). - (b) File, CSV, ICS import and OS APIs; then IMAP and CalDAV; then OAuth providers; then bank feeds through an aggregator. Each step adds reach and adds verification burden, cost or legal flux. - (c) Integration first. Fits the manager; needs accounts many users lack; widest attack surface. - Hypothesis: a budget capability should work from manual import before any live feed (05 Q7). - [CONFLICT: local-first, privacy] Live integrations widen the attack surface and bring third-party data in bulk; OAuth distribution requires vendor review (05 F20).

2.2 Respecting the user's own structure (01 F5) - Hypothesis: treat the user's own labels and organisation as first-class rather than imposing a taxonomy. A lead only: the supporting result concerns files, not obligations.

Theme 3 - Cross-domain synthesis and proactivity

3.1 Default stance (03 DQ1, Q1; 01 design question 4; 06 section 3.1) - (a) Reactive only. Safest; loses the "Jarvis" feel and some joined-up warnings that have measured money value (03 F13). - (b) Opt-in whitelist of low-regret facts: hard deadlines, conflicts, birthdays. The tracks' centre of gravity. - (c) Broad proactive synthesis. Highest ceiling; highest creepiness and shame risk; no evidence of value. - [CONFLICT: honest "can't"] (c) if inferences are presented as facts.

3.2 What may trigger a proactive message (03 DQ2, F9; 04 F10) - (a) Only user-entered or user-confirmed facts plus arithmetic on them. Verifiable and explainable; misses subtle patterns. Also keeps untrusted content out of proactive output (06 section 3.2). - (b) Model-inferred patterns too, each with a "why am I seeing this" link and a confirmation before first use.

3.3 When and how to deliver (03 DQ3, F2, F3, Q6, Q8) - (a) When the user next speaks to her (a free, perfect boundary); risks missing a deadline while away. - (b) A user-scheduled daily digest. Safest timing shape; content rules still apply. - (c) Real time, reserved for penalty-bearing deadlines. Needs an urgency model. - Voice: text-first for proactive items, voice only on request (hypothesis; no direct evidence). - [CONFLICT: local-first, honest "can't"] A desktop that may be off cannot keep a real-time promise; a reminder she cannot deliver is worse than none (03 Q6).

3.4 Money messaging (03 DQ4, F10, F14-F16, F19, Q12; 01 F9-F11, Q5; 02 design question 9; 04 F15) - (a) Facts only, neutral, no evaluation. (b) Framed against goals the user set. (c) Advice. Wave 1 and track C argue against (c). - Sub-questions: exact remaining-budget figures, or ranges and rolling windows (03 F14)? Money talk off by default and opt-in, or on with careful framing (02 design question 9)? What does she do when the situation is bad: organise facts and point to human services, avoiding debt advice (03 Q12)? - Hypothesis: retreat when the user stops opening the finance topic (03 F15); show the calculation rather than steering (03 F17).

3.5 Cross-domain permission model (03 DQ5, F7; 01 design question 5; 05 DQ3) - (a) One global switch. (b) A per-domain-pair matrix (budget-to-calendar yes, contacts-to-budget no), with conservative defaults and a prompt on first use. (c) Ask per message (prompts decay, 03 F22). - Trade-off: (b) is the natural expression of the brief's per-domain scoping and of contextual integrity, but costs setup and is expensive to explain (05 Q8). - [CONFLICT: privacy against the product thesis] Stronger walls reduce blast radius and reduce synthesis.

3.6 Being wrong gracefully (03 DQ6, F20-F23, Q2, Q5, Q9; 01 design question 7) - Common ground: show inputs; one-tap "wrong / not now / never this kind"; no repeat after a dismissal; a cross-domain mute for a person or life chapter; the first instance of a kind of observation is a question, not an action. - Open choices: after N dismissals, stop that category automatically or ask? After one ignored reminder, switch from "remind" to "drop, move or break it down?" - Hypothesis: tell the user what she does not watch for, and when she last checked (03 F21; 01 Q4).

3.7 Building the evidence the thesis lacks (03 DQ8) - Ship two or three narrow synthesis types behind a flag, measure dismissals and corrections, then widen. Costs time; produces the only data in this area.

Theme 4 - Conversational behaviour

4.1 Length and register (04 design question 1, F1-F4, Q2; 02 design question 8, Q3, F4) - (a) Fixed policy. (b) Per-user preferences learned from corrections. (c) Both, with explicit override. - Per-situation defaults (hurry mode for hands-busy contexts) rather than per-user; a visible simple/full toggle as in wave 1. - Trade-off: (b) and (c) need an inspectable preference store, and stored preferences go stale.

4.2 Ask or assume (04 design question 2, F5-F7) - (a) Ask only for side-effecting or money actions; otherwise answer with a stated assumption. (b) Ask whenever a detector flags ambiguity (detection is poor, 04 F5). (c) Never ask. - Trade-off: (a) needs a taxonomy of action classes (it lines up with wave 1's reversibility matrix, 00-summary.md item 6).

4.3 Provenance (04 design question 3, F9, F10, Q3, Q11) - (a) Always tag personal facts. (b) Only when asked or surprising. (c) Tag in text; speak only non-obvious sources. - Hypothesis with more weight than the format: a mechanical check that each personal claim exists in what the tool returned. Separate retrieved, derived and unknown; never state a personal fact from model knowledge. - Ties to wave 1's provenance lineage for memory (00-summary.md item 7).

4.4 Where honesty rules live (04 design question 4, Q10; 02 section 3.4) - (a) In the baseline only, inherited. (b) Harnesses may add rules but not remove them, with a short list of non-overridable behaviours (honesty, provenance, the "can't" floor). - Trade-off: less freedom for specialists, in return for a fixed envelope.

4.5 Drafting and summarising (04 design question 6, F17, Q9) - (a) Mirror the user's voice and position and show what changed (the evidence favours this as default). (b) Offer improvements proactively. - Open: summarising or translating a third party's message is processing their words (privacy decision needed, 04 Q9).

4.6 Repair and long threads (04 design question 7, F7, F18, F19, Q7) - Start with a fixed short pattern (own it in one clause, fix it) and check telemetry. Restate the working understanding in long threads; after a correction, rebuild from the whole corrected request.

4.7 Stress and vulnerability (04 Q8; 03 F10, Q4) - Hypothesis: when stress is evident, shorten, drop options, do the task, and suppress unsolicited cross-domain commentary, except a hard deadline with a financial penalty, mentioned once in plain language. Crisis handling is wave 1's territory (00-summary.md item 10).

Theme 5 - Harnesses

5.1 What a harness is keyed to (02 design question 1, Q1) - (a) Occupation (easy to explain). (b) Task or situation (fits the evidence, allows overlap). (c) Both. Several may be active at once. - Trade-off: more harnesses and a harder discovery problem.

5.2 How a harness loads (02 design question 2, section 3.5) - User-initiated (predictable; low-literacy users may never do it); profile-inferred (smooth; silent behaviour change and mode errors); task-triggered with a visible badge (middle path). Wave 1's mode-error warning applies to the first two.

5.3 What is always baseline (02 design question 3, section 3.2, F4, F6; 03 Q7) - Candidates: capture, calendar (including rotas and seasons), drafting, chasing, cash-flow awareness, document explanation, verbosity and modality control, scam awareness, honest scope. - Trade-off: a fat baseline is more to test and a bigger always-on surface; a thin one hides common needs in harnesses few will find. - Open conflict (06 section 3.3): scam defence and cash-flow awareness in the baseline against track C's tiny baseline proactive set.

5.4 Regulated and expiring knowledge (02 design question 4, F7) - Options: shipped with "valid as of" and expiry; fetched from official sources; supplied by the user. - [CONFLICT: local-first, privacy] Fetching rules leaks context and adds an outbound channel; shipped rules go stale.

5.5 Supply chain (02 Q7; 05 DQ8, Q10, F9) - First-party only; signed community harnesses with a permission manifest declaring which domains they may read and write; unreviewed. - Trade-off: ecosystem growth against the OpenClaw outcome. Wave 1 proposed sandboxing harnesses with user-approved handoff cards (00-summary.md item 5). - [CONFLICT: narrow tools, privacy] Unreviewed harnesses carrying third-party data are untrusted content inside the trust boundary.

5.6 Scam defence (02 design question 10, F6, persona 2.5; 05 F10) - Always-on guidance when a message or call looks like a scam, saying why. - Trade-off: false alarms; reading incoming messages is a privacy surface. - [CONFLICT: privacy] It puts untrusted content into the session by design; it needs the trifecta controls in 6.2.

5.7 The cost of being wrong differs by harness (02 Q8) - Hypothesis: the "can't" line and the confirmation threshold should sit in different places for a quoting harness, a medication harness and a subsidy-deadline harness.

Theme 6 - Security and privacy

6.1 How untrusted content touches the database (05 DQ1, F11) - (a) Read-only for untrusted-origin content; writes need confirmation. (b) Writes allowed but tagged by provenance and quarantined from instructions. (c) Free writes. - Trade-off: (a) taxes flow (capturing a job from a stranger's text becomes a confirmation); (c) is the memory-poisoning path. - [CONFLICT: privacy] (c).

6.2 Break the lethal trifecta by construction? (05 DQ2, F10; 06 section 3.2) - No outbound action in any turn that has ingested untrusted content; outbound only through a user-confirmed, narrowly typed tool; or accept the risk with logging. - Trade-off: constrains "send this for me". Matches narrow tools well. - Note: a cloud model call is itself an outbound channel (06 section 3.2).

6.3 Per-domain scoping of storage (05 DQ3, F12; 01 design question 5; 03 DQ5) - One key with software permissions; per-domain keys unlocked when needed; separate tables never joined without opt-in. Middle path: synthesis locally, only the minimal derived fact to the cloud. - [CONFLICT: privacy against the product thesis]

6.4 Cloud path (05 DQ4, F14, F15, F23; 02 design question 5; 04 design question 5) - Tokenise and send (current); tokenise and drop high-sensitivity classes; require a local model for those classes. - [CONFLICT: privacy] Tokenisation is minimisation, not anonymisation (05 F15; wave 1 00-summary.md item 2). Most real users' hardware will force the cloud path (05 F23), so the privacy promise is weakest for the target user (06 section 3.4). A legal-basis review for cloud processors is needed.

6.5 Hardware tiers, told honestly (05 DQ5, F8, F23) - Define tiers (cloud-only; small local model for routing and extraction; larger local model for everything) and tell the user which tier they have. Precedent: deterministic parsing first, LLM only if needed. - Trade-off: complexity against an honest "can't do that here", which supports the honesty constraint.

6.6 Sensitive fields inside third-party records (02 design question 5, Q4; 03 Q3) - Store customers fully; store minimally; store references with sensitive fields (an alarm code, a health note) held separately under a stricter rule. Use third-party data only for its stated purpose. - [CONFLICT: privacy] Against the hard constraint as written, since tokenising names and numbers is weak protection for such fields.

6.7 Keys and backups (05 DQ6, F22, F13) - User passphrase; OS keystore; hardware-bound where available. Encrypted backup export in a documented format. - Trade-off: usability for users with low digital literacy against recoverability; a lost key loses the whole life record. Encryption at rest covers disk theft only.

6.8 Telemetry in end-user builds (05 DQ9, F16; 03 Q11, DQ7; 04 Q1, design question 5) - Metadata only; opt-in content; none. Decide also how long proactive-message signals are kept. - [CONFLICT: privacy] The project rule "the answer is in her telemetry" is sound at dev time, but if shipped unchanged it makes the developer a controller of contacts and finance data, and logs the inferences track C warns about. Using a cloud model as an eval judge sends personal turns out.

Theme 7 - Lifecycle

7.1 Forgetting and archiving across domains (01 Q7; 02 Q6; 03 F23) - One control to scope, pause, archive or forget a person or life chapter across calendar, contacts, notes and budget. Harnesses unloadable; old professional data archived and not suggested. Ties to wave 1 00-summary.md item 7.

7.2 Leaving and surviving (05 Q5, F1, F3, F8, F22) - Open export format and no required cloud service, so the user can leave and the data outlives the developer.

7.3 Death and incapacity (05 Q9) - Estate access and power of attorney for a whole-life store. Unresearched; needs at least a position.

Theme 8 - Evaluation

8.1 What "excellent" is measured against (04 Q1, design question 5, design question 8, F11, F13; 03 F8, DQ7; 05 F24) - A private rubric set from real turns, scored on separate axes: length fit, groundedness, asked when needed, did not ask when not needed, honest disagreement, correct "can't". Options: hand scoring; LLM-as-judge; both with spot checks. - [CONFLICT: privacy] A cloud judge; a local judge is the clean option where hardware allows.

8.2 Testing with people who will not sit in a lab (02 design question 11) - Field diary studies; paired trials with trade bodies; synthetic personas (cheap, but they are the hypothesis layer).

Theme 9 - Legal review before any external user

(05 DQ10, F16-F21; 02 design question 6) - Jurisdictions (EU, UK, US states); role under GDPR; health positioning (FTC Health Breach Notification Rule); finance positioning ("not financial advice", not a credit tool); EU AI Act classification (unresearched). Any harness that judged other people's creditworthiness would change the AI Act class (05 F21, low confidence).

Quick wins that need no design session

Only items where the evidence clearly points one way and no design trade-off is involved:

  1. Do not tune or judge her behaviour on satisfaction signals alone. Thumbs-up and approval reward agreement (04 F11: Sharma et al. D1, D2; the GPT-4o rollback D14), and continued use is not endorsement (03 F8; 05 F24).
  2. Add two regression cases to whatever eval exists: "are you sure?" pushback must not flip a correct answer, and a correct "I can't tell from what I have" scores above a plausible guess (04 F11, F13, Q4). These are tests, not design changes.
  3. A "can't" reply always says what she can do instead, or where the user could go. This is the published floor of a major lab (D6) and matches Google's guidance (D8) (04 F16).
  4. Spoken replies carry no markdown, tables or long lists, and front-load the answer. Vendor guidance is concrete and consistent here (04 F3, D8, D9), though it is guidance for a discontinued product, not a study.
